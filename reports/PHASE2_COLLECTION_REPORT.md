## A. Collection overview

The frame is the **defined OpenAlex AI/CV primary-subfield corpus**, not all
global AI research: works dated 2020-01-01 through 2025-12-31, of type `article`,
`review`, or `preprint`, whose primary topic is in subfield `1702` or `1707`.
The primary population and detailed sample require `is_retracted:false`.
Separate all-work aggregates are retained for retraction sensitivity analysis.
The exact primary population filter (with `group_by=publication_year` for the
aggregate) was:

```text
from_publication_date:2020-01-01,to_publication_date:2025-12-31,primary_topic.subfield.id:1702|1707,type:article|review|preprint,is_retracted:false
```

Each sampled year replaced the two date bounds with that year's January 1 and
December 31; no other corpus clause changed. The 180-work test used
`per_page=25` in separate `openalex_smoke` directories, while production used
`per_page=100`.

## D. Interpretation and limitations

The full aggregate series and the detailed server-side sample answer different
questions. Citation totals are age-dependent and are **not** scientific quality
scores. Impact analyses should account for publication year and field; FWCI
comparisons must explicitly handle the complete FWCI absence among preprints.
Collaboration analyses based on institutions or countries will undercount works
with missing affiliation metadata, especially preprints. OpenAlex topics are
model-inferred and can assign surprising primary topics; a small test sample
included a face-mask paper inside this approved AI/CV frame. This is a taxonomy
limitation, not permission to silently change the corpus after collection.

OpenAlex-native topic membership and custom LLM/Multimodal/RAG text rules are
different analytical layers. Their counts are not directly equivalent. Title and
abstract rules also miss works without abstracts and may flag incidental mentions.
The frame omits AI-related work whose primary topic lies outside subfields 1702
and 1707. OpenAlex does not provide snapshot isolation; a completed run is a
time-bounded extraction rather than a frozen source-side snapshot.
